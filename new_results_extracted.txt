[P0] SECTION 4: EXPERIMENTAL RESULTS AND COMPARATIVE ANALYSIS
[P1] Evaluation of Topology-Aware Positional Semantic Divergence (PSD) Across Multi-Scale LLMs and Long-Context Regimes
[P2] This section presents the comprehensive empirical evaluation of the Topology-Aware Positional Semantic Divergence (PSD) framework for structured Data-to-Text (D2T) generation. The framework is systematically benchmarked against standard Parameter-Efficient Fine-Tuning (Baseline LoRA) across three sequence context lengths (L = 256, 1024, and 2048 tokens), six contemporary open-weights large language model families (Gemma 3, Llama 3.x, Qwen 2.5, Mistral, and Phi-3.5) spanning 3B to 9B parameters, and dedicated hyperparameter and architectural ablation studies conducted on an NVIDIA H100 PCIe (80 GB VRAM) platform.
[P3] 4.1 Long-Context Sequence Scaling (L = 1024 and L = 2048 Tokens)
[P4] A critical bottleneck in autoregressive text generation from complex, serialized structured data (e.g., multi-row Wikipedia tables and dense knowledge graph triples) is context degradation. As sequence length expands from standard 256 tokens to 1024 and 2048 tokens, standard attention distributions suffer from attention dispersion—entropy increases, and intermediate attention heads lose focus on structurally bound entities. PSD directly counters this degradation by penalizing attention divergence from geodesic graph shortest paths.
[P5] Table 1 outlines the results across the top three 3B/4B architectures (Gemma-3-4B, Llama-3.2-3B, and Qwen-2.5-3B) under both sequence budgets. The empirical advantages of PSD become increasingly pronounced as sequence length scales:
[P6] Gemma 3 (4B) at L = 2048: Achieves the highest overall fluency and recall in the benchmark, surging to 27.47 BLEU-4 (+4.77 points, +21.0% relative improvement over baseline) and 65.06 ROUGE-L (+6.68 points), with factual precision reaching 88.18%.
[P7] Qwen 2.5 (3B) at L = 2048: Breaks two landmark thresholds—factual claim precision exceeds 90% (90.31%), and hallucination rate drops below the 10% barrier to 9.69% (compared to 20.00% at L = 1024 baseline and 32.98% at L = 256).
[P8] Llama 3.2 (3B) Structural Stability: Maintains high factual precision (87.82%) and low hallucination (12.18%) at L = 2048, confirming that topological regularisation stabilizes generation across distinct pretraining inductive biases.

[P15] 4.2 Hallucination Suppression and Claim-Level Factual Grounding
[P16] Standard lexical metrics (BLEU, ROUGE) measure textual n-gram overlap but fail to distinguish between fluent truthful text and fluent hallucinations. To address this, an automated claim-level factuality evaluator extracts subject-relation-object semantic tuples from the generated text and verifies whether each extracted claim is topologically and semantically entailed by the input table or graph.
[P17] Figure 3 highlights the cross-architecture reduction in hallucination rate under PSD supervision. The results demonstrate that PSD acts as a powerful factual regularizer across architectures of vastly different depths and sizes:
[P18] 1. Llama-3.1 (8B): Baseline fine-tuning exhibited severe hallucination (41.48% hallucination rate) because the larger parameter count over-relied on memorized pretraining knowledge. PSD re-anchored attention heads to the input graph, driving hallucinations down to 17.44%—a massive 58.0% relative reduction.
2. Qwen-2.5 (3B): Hallucinations steadily decreased from 32.62% (L=256) to 12.16% (L=1024) and reached a project-wide record low of 9.69% at L=2048.
3. Gemma-3 (4B): Cut hallucinations from 23.02% to 16.07% at standard context and reached 11.82% at L=2048.

[P43] 4.6 Summary of Key Technical Insights
[P44] The empirical findings across sequence lengths, model families, and ablations establish several core insights:
[P45] 1. Resolution of Attention Dispersion in Long Sequences: The dramatic surge of Gemma-3-4B at L = 2048 (from 58.38 to 65.06 ROUGE-L) and Qwen-2.5-3B (reaching 90.31% factual precision) confirms that as the attention context budget quad-scales, standard self-attention requires explicit topological regularization to prevent semantic entropy.

2. Middle-to-Deep Layer Specialization: The layer placement ablation clearly demonstrates that supervising intermediate and deep layers (layers 9 through 34) yields the highest factual precision (up to 89.05% on LLaMA-3B). Early transformer layers handle low-level lexical syntax and token identity, whereas relational graph binding takes place in middle/deep layers.

3. Zero Inference Latency Overhead: Because the PSD loss function is applied strictly during training to regularize the LoRA adapter weights, the trained model operates with identical inference latency, zero added FLOPs, and zero extra decoding memory compared to a standard base LLM.
